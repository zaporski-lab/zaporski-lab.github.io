What do we do?

Our lab pushes the frontier of experimental quantum metrology using atoms and light. We turn atoms into precise timekeepers and sensors for elusive physical phenomena, while light probes the atoms and mediates non-classical atomic dynamics between them. To enhance this atom–light interaction, we couple atoms to an optical cavity: a pair of highly reflective mirrors that bounce light back and forth through the atomic cloud. Carefully engineered, this interface lets us suppress a fundamental source of measurement uncertainty: quantum projection noise.

Our near-term goal is to push this noise suppression to a record-breaking degree by generating a deeply entangled state of thousands of atoms. We aim to apply this entanglement directly to improve the stability of an optical lattice clock, and to search for clock-frequency excursions arising from physics beyond Standard Model - in particular, violations of Lorentz invariance (LIV). A defining feature of our approach is an unconventional choice of atomic species: thulium-169. Compared with established optical-clock atoms like ytterbium and strontium, thulium sidesteps several of their key limitations, while offering a uniquely versatile energy-level structure and heightened sensitivity to LIV.

Beyond enabling deep entanglement and new-physics searches, thulium is also a promising candidate for delivering laboratory-grade timekeeping outside the lab. We’re building an active optical frequency standard around it - and, in the long run, working to make it transportable.
